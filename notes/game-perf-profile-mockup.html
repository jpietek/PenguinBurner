A tiny fingerprint rides every game row. Click one and it opens a per-game detail: a MangoHUD-style frametime graph and the operating orbit. Recorded by the daemon into one small binary ring — all metrics, plus every frame's frametime, over a rolling 30 minutes.

The icon that says how a game runs

At 30 px the fingerprint isn't for reading exact values — it's a shape you recognise: a fat lopsided kite is a heavy GPU-bound game, a thin spike is something CPU-bound and light. It sorts and scans at a glance, and it's the click target into the detail.

Frametime, not FPS
Milliseconds expose stutter an FPS average smooths away. The 1%-low is just the p99 frametime.
Why keep clock
Off the fingerprint, but the power↔clock path is the only place undervolt/adaptive shows.
Cost
Frametime graph wants the raw frame ring; orbit wants the 1 Hz ring. Both already recorded.
The recorder — binary layout & volume

One ring file per game, written by burnerd

Two fixed-stride rings behind a small header. The 1 Hz record carries every published metric plus that second's frametime percentiles; the frame ring is one companded byte per frame.

FILE = header(64B) + metrics_ring[1800] + frametime_ring[N]

metrics record — 32 B/s      frametime entry — 1 B/frame
  clock        u16              u8 companded, 0.25 ms step
  mem_clock    u16              range 0–63.75 ms; spikes clamp
  voltage      u16              (exact tail kept in ft_p999)
  power        u16
  gpu_util     u8            volume, 30 min @ 1 Hz + per frame
  cpu_util     u8              metrics  1800 × 32 B  =  56 KB
  cpu_thread   u8              frames @120fps  ×1 B  = 216 KB
  fan          u8              frames @240fps  ×1 B  = 432 KB
  temp         u8              ─────────────────────────────
  uv_offset    i16             TOTAL   ~0.27 MB  →  ~0.48 MB
  present_fps  u16 (0.1)       on disk, zstd        ~0.15 MB
  framegen_fps u16 (0.1)
  latency      u16 (0.05ms)  20 games  ~6–10 MB live · ~3 MB disk
  disp_latency u16
  flags        u8  (fg/adaptive + tier nibble)
  ft_p50/p99/p999  u16×3   ← 1%-low & 0.1%-low, exact

burnerd owns the write — and it's essentially forced. Python only sees the 1 Hz snapshot; it can't observe per-frame frametimes without re-plumbing the socket. The daemon already holds frame markers at frame rate and already knows game start/stop, so it opens, appends, and rotates the ring. Keep it a dumb fast recorder — all interpretation (DNA normalisation, graph rendering) stays in Qt, which just mmaps and reads.
Cold start. A never-run game has no ring — no fingerprint, "warming up" until 5 min of our own capture. ProtonDB is the only estimate for un-run games — a separate feature, out of scope here.
