03 / Evidence & interpretation

Less abrupt, not perfectly linear.

The sharpest slope change falls in every tier. All six curves were already monotonic: there are no downward frequency steps to remove. The improvement is in how quickly frequency rises and how abruptly that rise changes.

Efficiency and Balanced still have a steep entry into their new ramps. Performance has the clearest reduction in its old local cliff. The intentional rising tails remain 2 / 4 / 4 bins in these attached profiles.

How the measurements work
  • Segment slope: Δclock / Δvoltage, in MHz/mV. Native voltage gaps are 5 or 10 mV, so raw MHz jumps alone are not a fair steepness measure.
  • Peak slope change: max |slope(next) − slope(previous)| across 125 internal vertices, in MHz/mV. Smaller means the worst local change in gradient is less abrupt.
  • Largest point-to-point rise: the largest positive Δclock between adjacent saved voltage points; the accompanying voltage interval is always shown.
  • Downward steps: count of adjacent points with a negative Δclock. No smoothing or interpolation is used for any metric.
  • Comparison: before and after use the same 127 voltages and the same stock reference values, checked directly from all six JSON files. Different selected operating points still affect shape.
What this comparison cannot establish

These JSON files describe saved curves and aggregate scan measurements. They cannot establish game frametime smoothness, long-term GPU stability, a controlled FPS/W gain, or whether every voltage point was independently stress-tested. The before profiles are from August 21 and 30; after profiles are from September 5, 2026. Only the after archive includes an explicit 60-second final-choice setting.
