01 Scan → select → verify → save
Main path · all requested tiers
__SCAN_SVG__

Tier setup: apply power and memory before measuring. Baselines are reused only with matching applied power, memory and tail. Performance descent reuse also requires matching baseline evidence within 10 mV / 15 MHz.

Missing GPU table: a finite undervolt still works; table-only clock work is skipped. Missing editable V/F support or a failed first baseline can prevent any new result.

Recovery shortcut: a selected interrupted-run checkpoint can bypass fresh baseline/descent and enter clock selection. Its direct final path has no normal interactive retry loop.

Owners: tier orchestration · final verification and saving. Critical errors at any probe abort further probing; ordinary later-tier baseline/target errors follow the skip-tier rule below.

02 Voltage descent: each step moves down, every exit is defined
One sweep · finite editable bins
__VOLTAGE_SVG__

Efficiency: descend once toward the floor, then rank eligible passing curves by measured FPS/W. Equal scores favor measured MHz, then lower power, then the earlier result.

Balanced: stop after the configured confirmation streak at a temperature-normalized FPS/W wall, once the required voltage drop is reached; keep the better passing point. Performance has no FPS/W descent wall.

Stop conditions: no lower legal bin, floor reached, cached unsafe pair, ordinary probe failure, or Balanced’s confirmed wall. Keep the passing starting point when there is no legal first step.

Owners: shared sweep · next lower bin · FPS/W policy.

03 Clock targets, higher-voltage retries and safe backoff
Finite rungs · fixed voltage ceiling
__CLOCK_SVG__

Lower custom MHz: at most ten steps at the proven sweep voltage, with no extra voltage descent. If the requested voltage was not reached, retain the proven voltage and report that fact. No passing point inside the clock limit means a target error.

At the voltage ceiling: no retry can exceed it. Keep a lower passing clock when the target is unsafe or cannot improve. Critical outcomes in retries and backoff abort the run too.

Power wall: an OC request over 22.5 MHz above measured clock needs measured power-limit or hardware-brake evidence to stop the climb; a loose software-power flag alone is insufficient.

Owners: custom targets · finite ladder · OC retries and scoring.

Checkpoint ≠ final verification. The passing flattened baseline and accepted descent selections are written durably. Some passing candidates remain in history until selection. Only a successful final soak archives a final verified profile; no valid baseline means there may be no new profile.
Crash recovery covers the first step. All candidate phases, including adaptive tiers, write an in-flight marker. A later run caches recognized probing markers with positive whole-number voltage and clock values; the old 5% voltage-drop and 95% baseline-clock gates are gone. Clean stops remove markers, and baseline/discovery phases are excluded. An abrupt ending does not establish its cause. A recovered marker stays until its durable blacklist write succeeds. Recovery rules.

Finite bounds: ≤3 tiers · strictly lower voltage bins · ≤10 custom down-clock steps · ≤10 main OC rungs plus finite higher-voltage retries · at most one hang-confirmation re-probe · final retries at strictly higher saved voltages. Default final durations are 60 / 180 / 300 seconds for Efficiency / Balanced / Performance; an explicit override replaces them.

Targets are curve anchors. Two rising tail bins add nominal 30 MHz of boost headroom. Requested mV/MHz can differ from loaded telemetry; power limiting can lower clock. Neither the anchor nor the tail is a hard voltage lock.

What changed

  • Same Advanced control order in all tiers: voltage, core MHz, memory offset, power limit.
  • Custom MHz bounds: Efficiency −15% to Balanced; Balanced from Efficiency to Performance; Performance up to +5%.
  • Removed clock-loss percentage gates. Workload failures, lost load, FPS regressions, and readback errors still reject candidates.
  • Exact tested curves survive selection and saving; matching tier baselines and descents can be reused.
  • Unknown GPUs use hardware bounds and a baseline-derived voltage floor; missing table targets preserve the passing undervolt.

Review fixes

  • One Efficiency sweep replaces repeated passes over the same bins.
  • Critical failures propagate through the whole adaptive scan.
  • All adaptive candidate probes leave recoverable crash markers, including shallow voltage steps and reduced clocks.
  • Blacklist checks use the new candidate clock, including measured gains.
  • Blacklisted Auto-OC backoff uses an editable bin within the voltage target.
  • Baseline fallback retains matching target labels and tested geometry; obsolete adjustment code and its tests were removed.

RTX 5080 · fresh verification

Quick verification only: each tier used 10 seconds of final testing: 8 seconds Q2RTX + 2 seconds CUDA. Search probes kept their normal duration. This verifies the scan/save path, not long-term game stability or every intermediate V/F point.

    Maintainer rules and limits

    Keep search steps finite and monotonic; preserve passing checkpoints when improvement stops. A critical GPU/workload error ends further probing. Never call an untested fallback stable, rebuild a selected curve, bypass the daemon for GPU writes, or silently exceed a custom target.

    No table entry is required for a finite voltage sweep: the fallback floor is 10% below loaded baseline voltage. An unknown GPU may still lack required driver access or fail baseline validation; report that reason rather than promising a verified profile.
